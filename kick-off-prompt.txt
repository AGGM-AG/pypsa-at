## 2. Topic — owner notes and actionables

Deduplicate these against the issue and the implemented features. Each item becomes a
requirement, a candidate, a non-goal or a `(pending Q-…)`. "Verify" means: check against code
on `main`, the 3H run, or a source before you write it down.

**Purpose and scope of the domain**

1. SF exists because the model can move carbon between borders and between states
   (atmosphere, `co2 stored`, sequestered, e-fuel carriers, solid carbon). The domain must make
   these carbon flows understandable and correct for synthetic fuels. Focus: CCU.
2. Carbon accounting itself (booking rules, budgets, ESR/ETS) is CA §4.14 and out of scope.
   In scope: base-year calibration of the CO2 flows that run through synfuel production, and
   making the flows traceable so CA can book them.
3. Answer explicitly: what is synfuel used for in the model per horizon, and is that the
   intended real-world purpose (e.g. aviation, shipping, chemicals vs. land transport or
   power)? Every unintended use becomes an acceptance criterion or a question.

**Carriers and buses**

4. Distinguish fossil oil from renewable liquids. Proposed starting point: fossil oil and
   renewable ("green") liquid fuels on separate buses, with one EU-wide copper-plate bus for
   the renewable side. Verify against the PyPSA-DE unravel pattern and decide whether it is
   reusable without being DE-centric.
5. Decide which carriers get their own unravelled buses, at least within Austria: oil and
   methanol at minimum; ask about NH3. For each unravelled carrier, state how it is
   transported once the EU copper plate is gone (road/rail, product pipeline, crude pipeline,
   none) and what transport data exists. Austria has a crude pipeline (AWP/TAL) to Schwechat
   and product pipelines to tank farms; verify.
6. Ethanol (AGRANA Pischelsdorf, AustroCel Hallein) is represented, but without a new
   bus_carrier. Map it to an existing carrier (oil, renewable oil, or methanol) and mix it
   there. Recommend the carrier with reasons (energy content, use in petrol blending). This
   replaces the "ethanol as its own carrier" intent of F-SF-04; reword the feature and answer
   Q-SPEC-49 accordingly.
7. Biofuels in the transport sector (biodiesel/FAME, HVO, bioethanol): calibrate the
   consumption side against Austrian statistics (BMIMI Biokraftstoffbericht, Energiebilanz).
   Production brownfield is F-BM-07 (link, see §5).
8. Mixing of fossil and synthetic/renewable fuels: allowed in transport and industry within
   configurable limits per horizon. The owner's note "Allow mixing of fossil and synthetic
   fuels based on …" is incomplete; ask what the basis is (blending quotas, RED III/ReFuelEU,
   technical blend walls such as E10/B7, cost).

**Trade and imports**

9. Find import/export statistics for e-fuels and biofuels for AT and, if possible, the EU, to
   calibrate 2025. Same approach as solid biomass trade in BM (F-BM-05): bound trade in the
   base year by statistics and give a sourced projection for later horizons.
10. Limit the volume of green imports of NH3, oil and methanol per horizon and raise their
    prices to sourced values (relates to H-T2-3, Q-P5-16; prices are TC §4.16, volumes here).

**Technologies (upstream and AT-specific)**

11. Check each production/conversion technology individually: Sabatier, methanolisation,
    Fischer-Tropsch, electrobiofuels, BtL, methane pyrolysis, Haber-Bosch, NH3 cracking,
    methanol-to-kerosene, methanol-to-power, DAC. For each: is the implementation right, is the
    cost/efficiency data reliable (source, year, version), and is the first year it may be
    built realistic?
12. Every one of them gets a first-technology-occurrence year (F-SN-05, Q-SPEC-14). Today
    many can be built in 2025, the base year, which produces the 2025 Sabatier and NH3 flows
    of §1. In the base year only existing plants run. SF supplies the years with sources; ask
    whether the list is written here or in the deferred scenario session.
13. Brownfield: existing production and conversion assets in AT and the EU are added from
    sources. Future buildout is allowed from sensible cost data and limited by configurable
    potentials per region. Is there a dataset for those potentials (e.g. CO2 point sources,
    sites, PtX potentials)? Research it.
14. Account properly for the CO2 bound in synfuel production where relevant (the carbon
    input of Sabatier, methanolisation, Fischer-Tropsch, urea) and calibrate 2025 against
    reality.

**Methane pyrolysis and solid carbon**

15. On-site plasma pyrolysis produces solid carbon. The owner note calls it "biochar"; verify
    the term (carbon black from fossil methane is not biochar from biomass; it matters for CA)
    and ask if unclear.
16. Does carbon black have a price or a disposal cost, in terms of the carbon it binds, even if
    the carbon stays bound? Ties to P5-06, P5-13, Q-P5-12, #89, F-SF-03.

**Regulatory interface**

17. How do e-fuels and biofuels work with the new carbon budget constraint that counts ESR
    emissions? Example: biodiesel produced in AT (supplying an EU green oil bus) and burned as
    oil in AT agriculture should be able to offset CO2 emissions. Is the solution to link the
    EU green oil bus to an AT oil bus, or something else? SF owns the flow structure that makes
    the origin traceable; CA owns the booking. Write the SF side, link to CA, and raise the
    question if the CA rules do not answer it.

**Validation**

18. Validate production, use, trade and CO2 flows against historical measurements for 2025
    (or the latest year available) at AT level, and EU level where cheap.

## 3. Completeness check — where does this list fail?

The notes above are not exhaustive. Ask under which circumstances they fail to depict the
2025 Austrian and European system of liquid fuels, synthetic gases and CCU, and extend the
domain where needed. State for each addition why the lists above did not cover it. Check at
least:
- The Schwechat refinery: crude input, product slate, refinery fuel gas, emissions, HVO
  co-processing, OMV pilot plants (ReOil chemical recycling, electrolyser), and how fossil
  refining is represented once AT has its own oil bus (Q-SF-D of the issue).
- Existing CCU in Austria in 2025: e.g. urea production from ammonia CO2 (Linz),
  CO2 recovery at bioethanol plants for beverage/industrial use, Underground Sun Conversion
  (RAG), C2PAT (cement CO2 to fuels). Real 2025 CCU volumes are small; the model must not
  produce large CCU flows in the base year.
- Methanol and ammonia used today in Austrian industry (owner IN/H2; SF supplies the
  carrier side).
- Non-energy use of oil (naphtha, bitumen, lubricants) and LPG.
- Oil in buildings and agriculture, aviation kerosene (bunker vs. domestic), inland shipping.
- RED III transport sub-targets and ReFuelEU Aviation quotas as optional constraints (RG owns
  them; SF must make the volumes measurable).
- Strategic oil stocks (are they relevant for the model, or a non-goal?).
- Other countries: what EU-level synfuel and biofuel brownfield exists (e.g. HVO plants,
  biodiesel plants, e-methanol plants) and does the model's 2025 EU production match it.

## 4. Sources

Record the retrieval date for each source. Prefer primary and statistical sources. Besides
Statistik Austria Nutzenergieanalyse, Energiemosaik and the datasets already used by
pypsa-at, research at least:
- BMIMI Biokraftstoffbericht
  (https://www.bmimi.gv.at/themen/mobilitaet/co2_monitoring/biokraftstoffbericht.html)
- Statistik Austria Energiebilanz (oil products, biofuels, non-energy use)
- Eurostat: energy balances (`nrg_bal_c`), oil and renewable commodity balances
  (`nrg_cb_oil`, `nrg_cb_rw`), imports/exports by partner (`nrg_ti_*`, `nrg_te_*`), SHARES
  (RED transport shares); Comext trade by HS code (biodiesel 3826, ethanol 2207, methanol
  2905 11, ammonia 2814)
- JRC-IDEES (latest edition) for EU brownfield and fuel use by sector
- Umweltbundesamt: Treibhausgasinventur / NIR (CO2 use, refinery and ammonia emissions),
  Biokraftstoffe reports
- EU ETS registry (EUTL) for installations and CO2 transfers
- Union Database for Biofuels (UDB), EASA/ReFuelEU reporting
- Fachverband der Mineralölindustrie (FVMI) annual report (Schwechat, product pipelines,
  storage)
- IEA Hydrogen Production and Infrastructure Projects Database (includes e-fuel and
  methanol projects); Hydrogen Europe Clean Hydrogen Monitor
- Cost and efficiency: technology-data / DEA Renewable Fuels, JRC, Concawe, Agora / Frontier
  PtX cost studies
- Potentials: PtX-Atlas (Fraunhofer IEE), CO2 point-source datasets (EU ETS, JRC), ENSPRESO
  where relevant
- Industry/lobby sources (eFuel Alliance, ePURE, EBB, Methanol Institute, plant operators):
  validation and plausibility only, never as the only source.

Name every source you are missing in your report.

## 5. Boundaries and non-goals

- Carbon accounting (booking, budgets, ESR/ETS, negative emissions): CA §4.14. SF writes the
  flow structure and base-year calibration only.
- Liquid biofuel production brownfield (biodiesel, HVO, bioethanol) and market introduction of
  BioSNG, bioH2, biomethanol, electrobiofuels, BtL: BM (F-BM-06, F-BM-07 on the BM v2 branch).
  Ask the owner where the split lies (recommended: BM owns production and feedstock; SF owns
  the liquid-fuel buses, blending, trade and consumption-side calibration).
- Hydrogen production, H2 imports and H2 transport: H2 §4.11. NH3: ask (H2, IN or SF).
- Demand for fuels (transport, aviation, shipping, industry, naphtha): TR §4.9, IN §4.8.
- Synthetic methane injection into the grid: GG §4.10 (SF owns production).
- Quotas and mandates as constraints: RG §4.15. Cost values: TC §4.16. First-appearance
  mechanism: SN (F-SN-05); SF supplies values.
- Visualisations: PE.

   in part; which carrier is still open).

5. Green imports of NH3, oil and methanol are volume-limited and priced from sources.
