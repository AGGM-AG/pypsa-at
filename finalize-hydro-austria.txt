The story to tell, in this order (each point one short paragraph plus the figure that shows it):
1. How the profile is built: `build_inflow_profile` takes the ERA5 runoff of the cutout
   (atlite, smoothed, lower-quantile threshold), aggregates it per model region and normalises
   it so that every region's profile sums to one over the weather year.
2. What the profile depicts: the *timing* of the water, not the amount. A June peak is not
   "much water", it is "a large share of the year's water arrives now". The cutout year decides
   when the snowmelt and floods happen; it does not carry a single MWh. Figure 04 panels a and c.
3. Where the amount comes from: the KLIEN catchment energy `E_current` (the study's
   Regelarbeitsvermögen, a long-term mean - state the reference period only if you can source
   it from the KLIEN material in `data/klien_potentials/` or the docs; do not write "20-year"
   unsourced), allocated to plants and regions, multiplied with the profile gives the hourly
   inflow in MW; for run-of-river `p_max_pu = inflow / p_nom` so that `p_max_pu x p_nom`
   is the hourly available power; peaks above `p_nom` are redistributed energy-conserving.
   Figure 02 (AT322, and AT121 for the redistribution).
4. Dry and wet years: the KLIEN energy is scaled per catchment by the E-Control annual
   generation of the weather year relative to the reference mean (Laufkraft for ror,
   Speicherkraft for reservoirs, natural PS inflow from E-Control), so the same shape can
   represent 2003 (dry), 2013 (model year) or 2024 (wet); figure 04 panel b and figure 03.
   Say in one sentence what the current run uses (2013, ror factor 1.07).
