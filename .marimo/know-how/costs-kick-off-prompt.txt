## Questions the notebook must answer

   (depreciation / amortisation) lifetime, or both at once? Show every place `lifetime` is used:

   (`build_year + lifetime`, brownfield phase-out in `add_existing_baseyear` / `add_brownfield`).
   Explain the consequence of one number serving both roles. Check where the values come from
   (technology-data version in `data/versions.csv`, `fill_values` default of 25 a, overrides in
   `data/custom_costs.csv`, `data/pypsa-de/custom_costs_nep_2023.csv` and
   `scripts/pypsa-at/build_custom_cost_at.py`).

2. **Cost attributes.** Define each attribute with its unit and how it enters the model:
   - `investment` (EUR/MW overnight, after the /kW -> /MW conversion),
   - `FOM` (% of investment per year),
   - `VOM` (EUR/MWh),
   - `fuel` (EUR/MWh_th) and `efficiency`,
   - `discount rate` (per technology, default 7 %),
   - `CO2 intensity` (t/MWh_th),
   - derived `capital_cost` = (annuity(lifetime, discount rate) + FOM/100) * investment * nyears,
   - derived `marginal_cost` = VOM + fuel / efficiency.
   Source: `prepare_costs` in `scripts/process_cost_data.py` and `calculate_annuity` in
   `scripts/add_electricity.py`. Explain the distinction between "raw" custom-cost attributes
   (overwritten before preparation) and "prepared" ones (`capital_cost`, `marginal_cost`, overwritten
   after). Note the special cases (e.g. `solar` capital cost copied from `solar-utility`, OCGT/CCGT
   fuel and CO2 intensity copied from `gas`, storage cost composition via `max_hours`).
   Are there any other levelised or quasi-levelised costs? Check for things like `standing losses`,
   `marginal_cost_storage`, `capital_cost` on Stores, and cost-free components.

     and CO2. Show the fuel cost: is gas priced via `marginal_cost` on the Link or via a Generator /

4. **The full cost workflow until the LP.** Draw the chain as a diagram:

11. **Degeneracy-breaking marginal costs.** `data/custom_costs.csv` gives solar, onwind, offwind and

12. **Shadow prices (dual values).** Explain what the solved network reports from the dual problem:

13. **Further aspects (short section, no deep dive).** Close with one or two paragraphs each on:
    - **Network expansion costs:** the `v1.25` transmission volume cap, length and underwater
      scaling of line and pipeline costs, and the overhead vs. underground setting.
    - **Implicit and zero costs:** free components, potentials (`p_nom_max`) as hidden cost limits,
      load shedding and the value of lost load if enabled, building retrofit costs on the demand side,
      and the absence of start-up costs because unit commitment is off.
    - **Myopic cost accounting:** each horizon optimises only its own year without foresight, so

    - **Comparing with LCOE:** levelised costs from revenues and costs per asset, and why they

14. **Unused PyPSA cost features (outlook, short section).** The PyPSA roadmap is posted in
    https://github.com/AGGM-AG/pypsa-at/pull/218#discussion_r4122150599 (read it with
    `gh api repos/AGGM-AG/pypsa-at/pulls/comments/4122150599`). Check each feature's status and the

    feature a very brief description and one or two concrete uses in PyPSA-AT:
    - **Piecewise linear costs (recently available).** A cost or efficiency curve made of several
      linear segments instead of one constant slope. Convex curves stay a pure LP. Non-convex ones
      need SOS2 or binary variables and turn the problem into a MILP. Possible AT uses: supply cost
      curves for limited resources (biomass and biogas potentials, tiered H2 or gas import prices),
      rising onshore wind or PV costs as cheaper sites run out (KLIEN potentials), and part-load
      efficiency of CHPs or electrolysers.
    - **Endogenous learning curves (planned).** Investment cost falls with cumulative installed
      capacity instead of following an external cost-per-year table. This is non-convex and needs
      piecewise linearisation or a MILP, and it only makes sense with foresight across horizons.

      myopic workflow, which PyPSA-AT uses.
